# Avenize Creative Studio

## Scope and authority
This skill applies only to Avenize client work in `docs/clients/avenize/`. It does not redefine River Ways' own brand, design system or agency acquisition engine.

Read before producing:
1. `docs/clients/avenize/README.md`
2. `docs/clients/avenize/DESIGN.md`
3. `docs/clients/avenize/APPROVAL_GATES.md`
4. The active campaign brief and asset register.

The exact client-supplied Avenize logo is canonical. Never redraw, trace, recolour, distort or replace it. Use its documented lime, silver, gold and obsidian identity. A design tool's generated palette or default theme never overrides this system.

## Production method
1. Identify audience, channel, funnel stage, campaign objective, message and CTA.
2. Check every product statement against a verified product capability and approved source.
3. Select the smallest useful format set; adapt one strong concept rather than inventing unrelated graphics for every channel.
4. Write copy before layout: headline, support, proof, CTA, destination and required disclaimer.
5. Design with editorial hierarchy, human context, deliberate negative space and premium restraint.
6. Use real client-provided product screenshots only. Use clearly labelled neutral placeholders in internal drafts where screenshots are unavailable; never fabricate an interface.
7. Record the source/editable file, export dimensions, intended channel, owner, status and approval.
8. Run identity, readability, contrast, crop, link and claim checks.
9. Do not publish, schedule, contact partners or spend money without the relevant client approval.

## Creative standards
- Premium, modern, globally relevant, confident and understandable to nontechnical business owners.
- Obsidian is the deep canvas; lime is the primary accent; silver and gold are meaningful supporting identity colours.
- Realistic, relatable business owners and teams; avoid uncanny AI people and generic tech stock imagery.
- Cinematic/editorial composition; glass and dimensional effects are restrained supporting elements.
- The first communication should explain a business problem before describing product architecture.
- Avoid overcrowded layouts, icon walls, generic AI motifs and unsupported superlatives.
- Every creative has one clear audience and one dominant action.

## Product truth
Never invent CRM, project management, finance, HR, automation, integrations, analytics or workflow capabilities. The client brief lists these as areas to explain only where verified in the actual product. Distinguish available, demonstrated, planned and unknown capabilities. Do not create fake metrics, testimonials, customer logos, pricing or endorsements.

## Video and motion
For each video provide: objective, target viewer, duration, hook, scene-by-scene storyboard, voiceover/on-screen copy, product footage requirements, sound direction, CTA, aspect ratios and edit notes. Product interface footage must be captured from the actual product. Provide captions and a no-audio comprehension path. Respect platform-safe zones and reduced-motion contexts.

## Presentation and print
Create a consistent master layout system. Verify slide narrative and evidence before decoration. Print files require confirmed dimensions, bleed, colour profile and QR destination. QR codes must be tested on a real device before release.

## Design tool use
UI UX Pro Max is a design-reference/search aid, not a source of brand authority. Anthropic frontend-design and canvas-design are guidance for distinctive implementation and original static artwork; review each upstream license and preserve required notices before copying any source files. Do not add dependencies or hosted APIs merely to make a visual draft. Prefer existing local tools and existing River Ways dependencies.

## Definition of done
A draft is not a deliverable. A deliverable has an editable source, final export, approved copy, verified product claims, correct dimensions, QA record, client approval and delivery/publication evidence. State explicitly what remains unverified.
