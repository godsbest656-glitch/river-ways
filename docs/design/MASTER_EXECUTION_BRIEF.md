# River Ways Website — Master Execution Brief
Version 1.0 · Primary delivery instruction for the AI design/development agent

## 0. The assignment in one sentence

Deliver the strongest feasible, distinctive, commercially effective, responsive, accessible, production-ready River Ways website in the shortest responsible time—working from the real codebase, making evidence-led decisions, preserving working business functionality, and verifying the actual result instead of stopping at a moodboard, plan, or attractive screenshot.

**This is a delivery assignment, not an open-ended design exploration.** Research exists to improve decisions and should be time-boxed. Make reversible decisions yourself. Escalate only genuinely consequential decisions that cannot be resolved from the brief or evidence.

## 2. The business outcome

River Ways is a creative, digital and Demand Engineering practice. Its website must help convert the right visitors into qualified business conversations. It is not a design-award exercise, a generic agency template, or an anonymous AI SaaS landing page.

The website must:
1. Make the River Ways proposition understandable within seconds.
2. Explain the business problems we solve and why our joined-up approach matters.
3. Show a credible method that connects strategy, creative, digital execution, demand generation and measurement where supported by the real offer.
4. Make services and engagement paths easy to understand.
5. Present real work and evidence honestly.
6. Give a qualified prospect a clear, low-friction next step.
7. Build trust with thoughtful content, deliberate art direction, reliable behavior and a coherent brand experience.
8. Support organic discovery through sound metadata, semantic structure, indexability and verified structured data.
9. Work reliably on mobile, desktop, keyboard and reduced-motion settings.
10. Give River Ways a credible platform that can evolve without a fragile rewrite.

The primary commercial test is: **Can the right prospective client quickly understand what River Ways does, why it is relevant to them, see evidence of judgment and execution, and confidently enquire?**

## 3. Brand truths and creative boundaries

- The brand name is **River Ways** (two words). The existing domain remains `riverwayse.com`.
- River Ways is the masterbrand. Demand Engineering, Demand Intelligence, services, intellectual property and future initiatives belong within its broader brand system.
- The ambition is an art-conscious, globally credible digital experience: intelligent, original, clear, expressive, commercially useful and technically disciplined.
- Treat the river as a strategic metaphor for direction, force, pressure, terrain, movement, confluence, adaptation and accumulated effect—not as a mandate to draw literal water.
- Water does not require a blue palette. Discover the right palette through research, comparison, contrast and application.
- Avoid generic agency templates, interchangeable gradient-led SaaS design, ornamental line art without meaning, repetitive card grids, arbitrary glassmorphism, fake product dashboards, and animation added only to impress.
- Avoid the previously rejected visual association with terracotta, near-black and warm parchment. Do not reproduce that look under a different name.
- Do not choose the logo, palette, type pairing, imagery system or visual route by habit. Compare real alternatives, recommend a route, then commit to a coherent direction.
- The cinematic hero, 3D/fly-over effect, scroll-driven card movement and layered composition are options—not requirements. Use them only if they clarify the story and justify their cost.
- Make the river concept useful even when no wave, water photograph or flowing line appears on screen.
- Internal principles of integrity, stewardship, dignity, order, service and excellence may guide decisions. Public expression must remain authentic and appropriate to the audience.

## 4. Evidence and honesty

Inspect actual source code, project history, live/preview behavior where available, and supplied business proof. Distinguish:
- **Verified:** directly observed in source, test, browser or authoritative source.
- **Recommendation:** the agent's reasoned decision.
- **Assumption:** a temporary premise.
- **Open decision:** genuinely requires owner input.

Never invent:
- Client names, logos, testimonials, awards or partnerships.
- Performance figures, growth metrics, conversion rates or business outcomes.
- Capabilities that are only planned or prototyped.
- Analytics findings that have not been retrieved and verified.
- Contact details, customer quotes or project results.

Use supplied portfolio evidence where relevant, but inspect the actual materials and describe each engagement accurately. When metrics are unavailable, explain the challenge, work performed, decisions and deliverables without fabricating outcomes. Do not imply that stock imagery depicts an actual client.

## 5. Deadline and operating tempo

Work in short, visible stages. Suggested time budget for a focused work session; adjust to the actual codebase and tool availability:
- 20–30 minutes: baseline audit and critical risks.
- 30–45 minutes: focused reference research and route comparison.
- 20–30 minutes: commit to a creative direction and establish core tokens.
- 60–120 minutes: build the highest-value complete page experience or representative implementation slice.
- 30–45 minutes: responsive, accessibility, conversion-path and technical verification.
- Remaining time: fix critical defects, document known limitations and prepare the release candidate.

These are planning limits, not a claim that every task can be completed within them. If a true blocker or substantial missing capability makes the full site impossible within hours, deliver the highest-value safe increment, report the blocker with evidence, and continue with the next feasible task. Do not pretend a production release is ready when it is not.

**Time-box research; do not time-box truth.** Never skip a security, data-loss, enquiry-capture, accessibility, or release-critical check merely to report completion.

### Decision rule
- Low-risk, reversible design choice: decide and proceed.
- Choice with several good alternatives: compare briefly, recommend one and proceed unless identity-level owner approval is explicitly required.
- Brand-defining choice: make a clear recommendation with evidence; record the decision and ask for approval when required.
- Production, privacy, cost, data-loss, security or irreversible infrastructure change: do not perform without explicit authorization.
- Missing access: continue locally or prepare a reviewable PR; do not fake a deploy or silently substitute another service.

Avoid long speculative status messages. Communicate useful milestones, blockers and evidence. Keep a written decision log so later agents do not repeat completed research.

## 6. Stage A — Baseline audit and protect the working system

Inspect:
- Framework, package manager, scripts, routes, page structure and CSS architecture.
- Existing branch/commit and uncommitted work.
- Current homepage and other important pages.
- Contact/enquiry form end-to-end behavior and notification/storage dependencies.
- Analytics initialization, event duplication risks, consent behavior and existing tracking.
- Metadata, canonical URLs, Open Graph, structured data, sitemap, robots and crawler directives.
- Responsive behavior, accessibility, image loading and performance.
- Existing fonts, image assets, case studies and their licenses/provenance.
- Hosting, build output, route precedence and deployment assumptions.
- Existing design work on branches or in docs that should be reused rather than recreated.

Produce a concise risk register. Label each item critical, high, medium or low and identify the evidence. Do not let an exhaustive audit become an excuse to postpone building.

**Protection rule:** do not change production DNS, registrar, nameservers, DNSSEC, email/MX/TXT records, domain routing, production deployment configuration, or unrelated lead/acquisition systems as part of this design assignment. Do not overwrite unrelated work. Do not merge or deploy without the applicable permissions and release process.

## 7. Stage B — Focused research, not an inspiration dump

Research a small but high-quality selection of references across:
- Exceptional digital agencies and brand studios.
- Editorial, cultural and art-directed websites.
- Architecture, industrial design, materials and spatial composition.
- Data storytelling and visual explanation of complex systems.
- Product UX, conversion flows and information architecture.
- Motion/interaction specialists and relevant implementation libraries.

For each reference record:
1. Direct URL and name.
2. The specific observed pattern worth learning.
3. Why it works in its original context.
4. How River Ways could reinterpret the principle.
5. What not to copy.
6. Any asset/license or implementation caveat.

Inspect actual responsive and interaction behavior where feasible. Use inspiration references already supplied in the project as starting points, not as a limit. Do not spend time compiling dozens of screenshots without decisions.

Deliver a compact reference atlas and move on.

## 8. Stage C — Compare creative directions and decide

Develop at least three genuinely different directions, initially:
- **Living Terrain:** context, adaptive systems, organic structure, layered depth and terrain-shaped movement.
- **Mineral Force:** pressure, weight, precision, sculptural typography, material contrast and engineered geometry.
- **Uncommon Intelligence:** editorial clarity, surprising scale, sharp hierarchy, evidence and confident visual restraint.

You may replace a route if evidence supports a better concept. Do not make the routes differ only by color.

For each route define:
- Strategic thesis and desired audience feeling.
- First-screen composition and hierarchy.
- Display/body typography direction with realistic copy.
- Color/material palette and tested contrast pairs.
- Image/illustration approach and asset feasibility.
- A visual explanation of Demand Engineering.
- Treatment of services, proof/case studies and enquiry.
- Motion/interaction behavior and reduced-motion alternative.
- Mobile adaptation.
- Strengths, risks, cost, performance and maintenance trade-offs.

Score routes consistently against:
- Brand distinctiveness.
- Comprehension within seconds.
- Commercial credibility and conversion.
- Flexibility of the masterbrand.
- Strength of storytelling and proof.
- Accessibility and responsiveness.
- Performance and implementation risk.
- Asset availability and maintainability.

Recommend one route. Explain the reasons briefly and clearly. Do not hold implementation hostage to endless exploration. Mark brand-defining approvals explicitly and continue with reversible work while approval is pending.

## 9. Stage D — Define the experience and content architecture

Build a visitor journey, not just a list of pages. For each major section/page document:
- Target visitor and question they need answered.
- Main message and proof required.
- Desired next action.
- Content/asset dependencies.
- Responsive and interaction requirements.

Validate the existing site structure before adopting this starting hypothesis:
1. Home — positioning, audience relevance, method, proof and primary CTA.
2. Services — clear offers, fit, deliverables and expected engagement shape.
3. Demand Engineering / approach — explain the joined-up method in plain language.
4. Work — selected, verified projects with context, contribution and evidence.
5. About / point of view — why River Ways works this way, only where it adds trust.
6. Insights / field guide — only if the content is useful and can be maintained.
7. Contact / diagnostic or consultation — clear expectations, minimal necessary fields, accessible validation and reliable submission.

Do not create empty pages to satisfy a sitemap. If time is limited, deliver a coherent homepage plus fully working critical conversion path, then finish other pages according to business value. Preserve valid existing URLs and avoid introducing soft 404s.

### Homepage narrative
The homepage should answer in order, with editorial flexibility:
- What is River Ways and who does it help?
- What problem or missed opportunity does it address?
- What is distinctive about its approach?
- What services or engagement path fit the visitor?
- What credible evidence supports the promise?
- What should the visitor do next?

Do not force a rigid section template. Use the chosen art direction to make the sequence feel authored and coherent.

## 10. Stage E — Implement the design system only as deeply as needed

Inspect the existing code and avoid duplicate systems. The current baseline has been described as React 18 + Vite 6; verify before relying on that fact.

Use a small set of explicit token layers:
1. Primitive values: colors, spacing, type sizes, radii, shadows, timing.
2. Semantic values: canvas, surface, text, muted text, border, accent, focus and status colors.
3. Component-specific values only when justified.
4. Components and patterns with accessible, documented behavior.
5. Page compositions with freedom for editorial variation.

Use CSS custom properties or the project's established equivalent. Establish:
- Typography scale, line heights, text measure and responsive heading rules.
- Spacing scale, layout widths, gutters and grid behavior.
- Palette and accessible state colors.
- Button/link/form styles and focus states.
- Navigation and responsive menu behavior.
- Section introductions, proof/case-study patterns, service explanations and CTAs.
- Motion tokens, easing and reduced-motion behavior.
- Image/media ratios, crop rules, loading behavior and fallbacks.

Avoid building a large component library before the visual direction is proven. Avoid arbitrary hex codes, one-off spacings, excessive dependencies and abstractions that add more complexity than value. Keep content maintainable and avoid burying important copy inside graphics.

## 11. Stage F — Build in order of business impact

Recommended implementation order:
1. Establish a strong, understandable hero with an unmistakable primary CTA.
2. Explain the offer and Demand Engineering approach in concrete language.
3. Make the service and engagement paths clear.
4. Present the strongest verified work as credible evidence.
5. Make contact/enquiry visible, accessible and reliable.
6. Complete navigation, footer, secondary pages and useful supporting content.
7. Apply motion and art-directed details after the content and interaction fundamentals work.
8. Refine responsive layouts, image art direction, typography and micro-interactions.
9. Complete SEO/metadata and analytics checks without duplication or regression.

If the current implementation already does one of these well, retain it and move to the next gap. The purpose is not to rewrite for the sake of rewriting.

### Hero requirements
The first screen must:
- Communicate a clear proposition, not only a poetic slogan.
- Have a visible, descriptive primary action and sensible secondary path if needed.
- Create a distinctive visual memory tied to the selected creative route.
- Remain readable and useful at common desktop and mobile sizes.
- Load gracefully when video, WebGL, animation, remote imagery or scripts are unavailable.
- Avoid hiding the core proposition behind an animation sequence or interaction.
- Avoid excessive initial bundle/media weight and layout shift.

### Motion requirements
Motion may express flow, direction, force, confluence, cause/effect or progressive understanding. Every motion has a purpose. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, keep content accessible in the DOM, avoid scroll hijacking and hover-only meaning, and test on touch devices. Do not let a cinematic effect jeopardize conversion or performance.

## 12. Stage G — Content and proof quality

Write clear, specific, human copy. Avoid generic phrases such as “we elevate brands” unless supported by a precise explanation. Explain what the work is, for whom, why it matters and how River Ways approaches it.

For every case study, use the evidence available:
- Client/project name and permission/context.
- The problem or opportunity.
- Scope and River Ways' actual contribution.
- Strategic/creative/technical decisions.
- Deliverables and verified outcome, if available.
- Media and source credits.

If outcome data is unavailable, state no metric and present the work honestly. Never make a prototype look like a launched client product unless clearly labelled.

Use licensed fonts, images, video, icons and code. Prefer optimized local assets when suitable. Record asset source, license, attribution and intended use. Do not use a random stock-photo gallery to create the appearance of scale.

## 13. Stage H — Accessibility, performance and responsive behavior

Accessibility and resilience are part of the design, not optional finishing work.

Minimum checks:
- Semantic landmarks and sensible heading order.
- Keyboard navigation through all links, menus, forms and interactive elements.
- Visible focus and focus that is not hidden by sticky UI.
- Accessible names, labels, instructions and validation feedback.
- Contrast meeting WCAG 2.2 AA targets.
- Usable zoom/reflow, text scaling and long-content behavior.
- Touch interactions and target sizes.
- Reduced-motion alternative and no essential information conveyed only by motion.
- Meaningful fallbacks when optional media/scripts fail.

Performance:
- Optimize image/video formats, sizes and responsive sources.
- Set dimensions/aspect ratios to reduce layout shift.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold media where appropriate.
- Avoid unnecessary client JavaScript, oversized libraries and blocking animation.
- Check network failures and console errors in the tested preview.
- Do not claim a Core Web Vitals score without actually measuring it.

Test at minimum a wide desktop, a laptop-sized viewport and a narrow mobile viewport. Browser automation or screenshots are evidence only for the view and conditions actually tested.

## 14. Stage I — Enquiry, analytics and SEO must not regress

Treat the primary conversion path as critical business functionality.

- Verify the form's actual endpoint, validation, success/error states and data storage.
- Verify that notifications are sent to the currently approved, accessible business inbox; do not assume old credentials or email addresses still work.
- Never expose secrets in frontend code or commits.
- Test the flow with safe test data and avoid sending real unsolicited messages.
- Inspect existing analytics before changing it. Avoid double-initializing tags or firing duplicate page views/events.
- Preserve consent/privacy behavior and do not collect unnecessary personal data.
- Track only meaningful events that can be accurately defined and verified.
- Preserve or improve titles, descriptions, canonical URLs, social metadata, structured data, sitemap, robots and crawler behavior based on the real deployment.
- Do not add fake schema properties, ratings, reviews or business facts.
- Verify canonical host and indexability against the actual target environment; a preview hostname must not accidentally become the production canonical.
- Do not modify DNS or production routing during design work.

If a live integration cannot be tested due to missing credentials or environment bindings, report it as blocked and give a precise verification procedure. A successful local build is not proof that the production form or analytics works.

## 15. Stage J — Code quality and collaboration

- Work on a topic branch and use small, meaningful commits.
- Inspect remote and local branch state before making assumptions.
- Preserve unrelated work and avoid broad, unreviewable rewrites.
- Follow repository instructions and existing lint/test/build conventions.
- Use semantic, maintainable React components and CSS.
- Avoid adding dependencies without a clear benefit and maintenance rationale.
- Do not commit secrets, private customer data or proprietary assets.
- Do not change production settings, merge or deploy without authorization and release requirements.
- Keep a decision log for major choices.
- Open a reviewable PR with a clear summary, preview, test evidence, risks and remaining work.

## 16. Mandatory acceptance gates

A page is not complete merely because it compiles.

### Gate 1 — Brand and comprehension
- The River Ways proposition is clear quickly.
- The chosen visual route is coherent and distinctive.
- The design avoids prohibited generic/rejected patterns.
- Content is accurate and evidence-backed.

### Gate 2 — Journey and conversion
- The visitor can understand the offer, see credible proof and find the next action.
- Primary CTA and navigation work.
- Forms handle validation, loading, success and failure accessibly.
- The primary enquiry path is tested where environment access permits.

### Gate 3 — Visual and responsive
- Desktop and mobile are intentionally composed.
- Real content lengths have been tested.
- Images, type, layout and motion feel like one system.
- No major overflow, clipping, unreadable text or layout breakage remains in tested viewports.

### Gate 4 — Accessibility and resilience
- Keyboard, focus, labels, contrast and reduced motion are checked.
- Core content works if optional media fails.
- No interaction depends only on hover or pointer precision.

### Gate 5 — Technical and operational
- Actual build and available tests pass, or failures are explicitly reported.
- No critical console/network error remains unexplained in tested flows.
- SEO and analytics behavior are inspected for regression.
- Asset licenses/provenance and secrets handling are checked.
- Deployment assumptions and preview URL are documented.

Mark each check **Pass**, **Fail**, or **Not tested**, with evidence. Do not turn unknown into pass.

## 17. Definition of completion

The goal is a production-ready release candidate, not just a concept. A release candidate must have:
- A coherent, implemented creative direction.
- A usable, responsive website experience for the agreed scope.
- Accurate, polished content and credible project proof.
- A working primary conversion path or an explicitly documented environment blocker.
- Consistent tokens and maintainable component patterns.
- Accessibility, performance, SEO and analytics checks recorded.
- A successful build and relevant tests, or explicit failure details.
- Preview evidence and a reviewable PR.
- A concise list of open decisions, known limitations and follow-up work.

If full scope cannot responsibly be completed in the available hours, prioritize in this order:
1. Prevent data loss, security defects and production regressions.
2. Make the primary visitor journey and enquiry path reliable.
3. Complete a distinctive, polished homepage and essential service/proof/contact sections.
4. Finish supporting pages and search/social metadata.
5. Add advanced motion and decorative enhancements last.

Do not claim the site is deployed or production-ready unless deployment and production behavior were actually verified.

## 18. Required final report

Return a concise but complete report:
1. **Outcome:** what is implemented and what is not.
2. **Creative decision:** selected route, rationale and remaining approvals.
3. **Visitor journey:** pages/sections and primary CTA.
4. **Files changed:** important source and documentation paths.
5. **Branch and PR:** exact names and URLs.
6. **Preview:** URL and commit tested, if available.
7. **Verification:** commands run, build/test results, browser viewports, accessibility checks, enquiry/analytics/SEO checks.
8. **Not tested:** anything requiring missing credentials, bindings, browser access or production access.
9. **Risks/blockers:** specific evidence and what is needed to resolve each.
10. **Next action:** the single highest-priority step.

Never state “done” when the evidence shows only a plan, a local code change or a successful build.

## Final instruction

Take ownership of the outcome. Understand the brand and the codebase, make sound decisions, and build. Be ambitious in the creative work and disciplined in the engineering. Move quickly without bluffing. The goal is a website River Ways can use to win trust and business—not merely a design that looks good in a presentation.
